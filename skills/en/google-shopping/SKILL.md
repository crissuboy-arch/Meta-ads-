# Google Shopping

This skill enables you to plan, configure, and optimize Google Shopping campaigns, from product feed creation to ROAS analysis and advanced strategies.

---

## Keywords

Google Shopping, Merchant Center, Product Feed, PMax Campaigns, Bid Optimization, ROAS, CPC, CTR, Bidding Strategies, Google Ads, Product Ads, SKU, GTIN, Product Attributes.

---

## Quick Start

1.  Create and configure your Google Merchant Center account, verifying domain ownership.
2.  Generate and submit a valid XML or CSV product feed to Merchant Center, ensuring all required attributes are filled in.
3.  Link Google Merchant Center to your Google Ads account to enable Shopping campaign creation.
4.  Create a Performance Max (PMax) campaign in Google Ads, selecting the "Sales" objective type and including the product feed as the inventory source.
5.  Set an initial daily budget (e.g.: $100.00) and a bidding strategy focused on "Maximize Conversion Value" with a Target ROAS (e.g.: 300%) to start optimizing returns.

## Core Workflows

### Workflow 1: Product Feed Creation and Optimization in Google Merchant Center

This workflow details the process of preparing, submitting, and optimizing the product feed, the foundation for any Google Shopping campaign.

*   **Step 1: Product Data Preparation.**
    Gather all the essential data for each product: `id` (unique SKU), `title` (descriptive title), `description` (detailed description), `link` (product page URL), `image_link` (main image URL), `price` (price with currency, e.g.: `99.90 USD`), `availability` (availability, e.g.: `in stock`, `out of stock`), `brand` (product brand), `gtin` (global barcode, e.g.: EAN/UPC), `condition` (condition, e.g.: `new`, `used`), and `google_product_category` (Google's standardized category).
    *   **Example:** For a smartphone, the `title` should be "Samsung Galaxy S23 Ultra 256GB Smartphone Black," not just "Phone." The `price` should include the currency `5999.00 USD`.

*   **Step 2: Feed Format Selection and Generation.**
    Select the most suitable feed format: XML (for large product volumes with frequent updates), CSV (for smaller volumes or spreadsheet management), or Google Sheets (for easy collaboration). For e-commerce with more than 500 products, XML is most recommended. Use e-commerce plugins (Shopify, WooCommerce) that generate feeds automatically, or third-party tools like DataFeedWatch.
    *   **Example:** An e-commerce store with 5,000 products should use an XML feed generated by a plugin or ERP system, with the feed URL being `https://www.mystore.com/product-feed.xml`.

*   **Step 3: Feed Submission and Scheduling in Merchant Center.**
    In Google Merchant Center, navigate to "Products" > "Feeds." Click the blue "+" button to add a new feed. Select the target country and language (e.g.: United States, English). Name the feed (e.g.: "Main Feed Store X"). Choose the "Scheduled fetch" input method and enter your XML/CSV feed URL. Configure a daily schedule for a low-traffic time on the site (e.g.: 3:00 AM ET) to ensure the feed is always up to date.
    *   **Example:** Set up a scheduled fetch for `https://www.mystore.com/product-feed.xml` at 3:00 AM ET, seven days a week.

*   **Step 4: Diagnostics Error Monitoring and Correction.**
    After submission, open the "Diagnostics" section in Merchant Center. Prioritize fixing disapproved items (critical errors that prevent ads from showing) and then warnings (issues that can limit performance). Common errors include "Inconsistent price," "Invalid image link," or "Missing GTIN attribute."
    *   **Example:** If a product is disapproved with "Price mismatch," review the `price` in your feed and on the site to ensure they're identical (e.g.: $199.99 in the feed and $199.99 on the site).

*   **Step 5: Feed Attribute Optimization for Better Performance.**
    Refine the `title`, `description`, and `google_product_category` attributes to improve ad relevance. Include relevant keywords in titles and descriptions, thinking about how users search. Use the `custom_label_0` through `custom_label_4` attributes for internal segmentation (e.g.: "high-margin," "summer-sale").
    *   **Example:** Change a `title` from "Dress Shirt" to "Men's Slim Fit Cotton Dress Shirt Navy Blue" to be more specific and attract qualified searches.

### Workflow 2: Performance Max (PMax) Campaign Structure and Optimization for E-commerce

This workflow guides the creation and optimization of PMax campaigns, the newest and most automated format for Google Shopping.

*   **Step 1: Performance Max Campaign Creation.**
    In Google Ads, click "+ New Campaign," select "Sales" as the objective. Choose "Performance Max" as the campaign type. Uncheck the "Find new customers" option if the initial focus is on remarketing or existing customers, or keep it checked if new customer acquisition is the priority.
    *   **Example:** Start a PMax with a "Sales" objective for a new fitness fashion e-commerce store.

*   **Step 2: Budget and Smart Bidding Setup.**
    Set a realistic daily budget for the campaign (e.g.: `$250.00`). For the bidding strategy, select "Maximize Conversion Value." For ROAS optimization, check the "Set a Target ROAS" box and enter a value (e.g.: `300%`). If the campaign is new and has no conversion history, start without a Target ROAS for 2-3 weeks to let the algorithm collect data.
    *   **Example:** An electronics campaign with a 350% Target ROAS to ensure every $1 spent generates $3.50 in revenue.

*   **Step 3: Strategic Asset Group (Asset Groups) Construction.**
    Create Asset Groups that segment products or logical categories. For each group, add multiple headlines (up to 15, short and long), descriptions (up to 5), images (up to 20, with varied sizes like 1200x1200 and 1200x628), videos (up to 5, optional, minimum 10 seconds), and a relevant final URL (e.g.: `https://www.mystore.com/smartphones`).
    *   **Example:** An Asset Group for "Samsung Smartphones" should have headlines like "Samsung Galaxy on Sale," "Samsung Smartphones with Discounts," descriptions about features, and images of different Galaxy models.

*   **Step 4: Providing Audience Signals.**
    Add audience signals to guide the PMax algorithm. Include your remarketing lists (e.g.: "30-day website visitors," "Abandoned carts"), customer lists (CRM uploads), and custom audiences (e.g.: "People who searched for 'best smart tvs 2024'"). This speeds up the campaign's learning phase.
    *   **Example:** Add a "Purchasers in the last 90 days" remarketing list and a "Running Shoe Enthusiasts" custom audience for a sports footwear campaign.

*   **Step 5: Continuous PMax Monitoring and Optimization.**
    Monitor campaign performance through the Asset Group and Insights reports. Pause low-performing asset groups (ROAS below target). Increase the budget for campaigns with positive ROAS and scale potential. Adjust the Target ROAS gradually (e.g.: from 300% to 320% if performance is consistent). Use "URL Exclusions" in the campaign settings to keep PMax from advertising out-of-stock or low-value products.
    *   **Example:** If the "Kids' Shoes" Asset Group shows a 150% ROAS against a 300% target, pause it and investigate the creatives or products included. If "Men's Athletic Shoes" is at 400%, consider increasing the overall campaign budget.

## Checklist

- [ ] Google Merchant Center account configured, verified, and with complete business information.
- [ ] XML/CSV product feed submitted, scheduled for daily updates, and free of critical errors in Diagnostics.
- [ ] Google Merchant Center linked to the Google Ads account.
- [ ] Conversion tracking configured correctly in Google Ads, including conversion value.
- [ ] Performance Max campaign created with a "Sales" objective and product feed selected.
- [ ] "Maximize Conversion Value" bidding strategy with a Target ROAS defined (or without one, if in the learning phase).
- [ ] Asset Groups segmented by category, brand, or profit margin, with multiple high-quality headlines, descriptions, and images.
- [ ] Audience Signals added (remarketing lists, customer lists, custom audiences).
- [ ] Final URL expansion disabled in PMax, if there are specific URLs to promote.
- [ ] Daily monitoring of Merchant Center Diagnostics to identify and fix new product errors.
- [ ] Weekly analysis of the PMax "Asset Groups" and "Insights" reports for performance optimization.
- [ ] URL exclusions for out-of-stock or low-performing products in the PMax campaign settings.

## Common Mistakes

1.  **Outdated or inconsistent product feed**: Leads to product disapprovals, wrong prices being shown, or ads for out-of-stock products, creating a bad experience and wasted budget. **How to avoid it**: Set up a daily feed schedule in Merchant Center and monitor the "Diagnostics" section regularly. Implement automation so feed information reflects the site's stock and prices in real time. Example: A feed that doesn't update the `availability` status when a product goes `out of stock`, resulting in paid clicks for an unavailable product.
2.  **Product images that violate guidelines**: Images with promotional text, logos, watermarks, or non-white colored backgrounds are often disapproved. **How to avoid it**: Use high-resolution images (minimum 800x800 pixels), with a pure white or transparent background, with no text, logos, or additional borders. Example: An image with the word "SALE!" in the top corner is disapproved, requiring a clean product image.
3.  **No Target ROAS in PMax campaigns after the learning phase**: Leaving PMax on "Maximize Conversion Value" without a Target ROAS for a prolonged period (after 30+ conversions) can lead to excessive spending on low-value conversions, hurting profitability. **How to avoid it**: After 2-3 weeks of data collection and at least 30 conversions, add a realistic Target ROAS (e.g.: 250%) based on initial performance to optimize profit and control spend.
4.  **Generic titles and descriptions in the product feed**: Titles like "TV" or "T-Shirt" don't provide enough information for Google or the user, resulting in lower relevance and CTR. **How to avoid it**: Create keyword-rich titles and descriptions, detailing brand, model, color, size, and relevant features. Example: Instead of "TV," use "LG OLED C2 55-inch 4K HDR Smart TV."
