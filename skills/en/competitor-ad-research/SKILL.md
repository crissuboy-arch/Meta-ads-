# Competitor Ad Research

This skill enables in-depth analysis of competitors' advertising strategies on Meta Ads, Google Ads, and TikTok Ads, revealing tactics for creative, copy, targeting, and sales funnels.

---

## Keywords

Meta Ads spying, Google Ads competitors, TikTok Ads benchmark, creative analysis, rival ad copy, competitor audience targeting, ad sales funnel, competitor ROAS, competitive CPC, market CTR, spy ads, ad libraries, bidding strategies.

---

## Quick Start

1.  Go to the Meta Ad Library at facebook.com/ads/library and search for the pages of direct and indirect competitors (e.g., "Company X Digital Solutions").
2.  Use the Google Ads Transparency Center for specific domains and Google Ads Keyword Planner for relevant keywords.
3.  Explore third-party tools like Semrush (Advertising Research) or SpyFu to get paid keyword data and competitor bid estimates.
4.  Capture examples of creatives (images/videos), copy, and Calls-to-Action (CTAs) from the active ads with the longest run time.
5.  Record inferred targeting patterns, promoted offers, and the landing page structure used by the competitors' ads.

## Core Workflows

### Workflow 1: Creative and Copy Deconstruction on Meta Ads

This workflow details the in-depth analysis of competitor ads on Meta Ads to identify winning patterns in creative and copy.

**Step 1: Identify Key Competitors and Pages**
*   List the 3-5 most relevant direct competitors and 2-3 indirect ones for the product/service. For a financial management SaaS, competitors might be: QuickBooks, Xero, FreshBooks, Wave.
*   Collect the exact Facebook/Instagram page names of these competitors.

**Step 2: Explore the Meta Ad Library**
*   Go to facebook.com/ads/library.
*   Select "All ads" and the target country (e.g., United States).
*   Search by each competitor's page name (e.g., "QuickBooks," "Xero").
*   Apply filters: "Media type" (All, Image, Video, Carousel) and "Ad status" (Active).
*   Sort by "Start date" to identify the oldest ads still active — a strong indicator of performance.

**Step 3: Detailed Analysis of Winning Creatives**
*   **Image/Carousel Creatives**: Observe the visual style, dominant colors, use of people (stock photos or real), graphics, testimonials, certification badges. Check for text on the image and its legibility. (E.g., QuickBooks uses clean images with smiling people and icons that convey financial organization, in blue and green colors.)
*   **Video Creatives**: Focus on the first 3-5 seconds (the "hook"). Analyze pacing, soundtrack, presence of voiceover, captions, and whether the product/service is demonstrated or merely illustrated. Identify whether they're filmed videos, animations, or screencasts. (E.g., Xero often uses short animated videos that simplify the complexity of management processes, focused on problem-solving.)
*   **Format**: Identify whether the competitor prioritizes feed, stories, reels, or Collection Ads.

**Step 4: Copy and CTA Dissection**
*   **Headline**: Analyze the value proposition and the emotional or rational trigger used. (E.g., "Bookkeeping Without the Bureaucracy," "Simplified Management for Your Business.")
*   **Primary Text**: Identify the pain points addressed, the benefits promised, and the product/service features highlighted. Observe the use of emojis, bullet points, social proof (testimonials, numbers), and text length.
*   **Call-to-Action (CTA)**: Check the clarity and urgency of the CTA. (E.g., "Learn More," "Try It Free," "Request a Quote," "Download the E-book.")
*   **Offer**: Infer what's being promoted (free trial, demo, e-book, webinar, discount coupon).

**Step 5: Infer Targeting and Sales Funnel**
*   From the language, the type of problem addressed, and the offer, infer the target audience (e.g., "Small businesses," "Freelancers and solopreneurs," "Accountants").
*   Determine the funnel stage the ad targets. CTAs like "Download the E-book" indicate top of funnel, while "Book a Demo" is bottom of funnel.
*   Example Conclusion: "Competitor QuickBooks uses image creatives focused on peace of mind and time savings, with direct copy for freelancers and small businesses, using a 'Learn More' CTA leading to a lead capture landing page. Xero, on the other hand, invests in explainer videos for a more sophisticated audience, with 'Try It Free' going straight to the product."

### Workflow 2: Keyword Mapping and Bidding Strategy on Google Ads

This workflow focuses on discovering competitors' paid keywords and their bidding tactics on Google Ads.

**Step 1: Identify Competitor Domains**
*   Collect the URLs of the main competitors advertising on Google Search (e.g., quickbooks.intuit.com, xero.com, waveapps.com).

**Step 2: Use Competitor Research Tools (Semrush/SpyFu)**
*   Open a tool like Semrush (Marketing > Advertising > Advertising Research) or SpyFu.
*   Enter a competitor's domain (e.g., quickbooks.intuit.com).
*   Navigate to the "Paid Keywords" or "Advertising Research" section.
*   Filter by keywords with the highest search volume or highest estimated paid traffic.

**Step 3: Analyze Keywords, Position, and Match Types**
*   Identify the 10-20 most relevant, highest-volume keywords competitors are bidding on (e.g., "business management software," "online accounting," "invoicing software," "ERP for small businesses").
*   Observe the average ad position for those keywords. A consistent 1st or 2nd position indicates aggressive bidding.
*   Infer the match types (broad, phrase, exact) the competitor may be using, based on the diversity of keywords triggered.
*   Detect competitor brand keywords (e.g., "QuickBooks," "Xero").

**Step 4: Estimate Budget and Bids (CPC)**
*   The tools (Semrush/SpyFu) provide estimates of the competitor's paid traffic and monthly cost.
*   Observe the average Cost Per Click (CPC) for the most contested keywords. (E.g., "business management software" might have a CPC of $8-$20.)
*   Identify whether the competitor focuses on high-volume, high-CPC generic terms or on lower-volume, higher-intent long-tail terms.

**Step 5: Analyze Landing Pages and Ad Messaging**
*   Click competitors' active ads in Google search results (carefully, so as not to distort bid data).
*   Analyze the landing page structure: message clarity, contact forms, CTAs, social proof (testimonials, client logos).
*   Check the landing page's relevance to the ad keyword (e.g., an ad for "online accounting" should lead to a specific page about that service, not the generic homepage).
*   Example Conclusion: "Competitor Wave dominates long-tail keywords like 'invoice software' with ads that lead directly to a specific product page and a 'Free Trial' CTA. QuickBooks, meanwhile, invests in brand and generic terms like 'online accounting,' with higher bids driving to a pricing consultation page."

## Templates

### Competitor Campaign Analysis Template (Meta Ads)

```
Competitor Campaign Analysis - [Competitor Name]
Analysis Date: 2024-03-15
Platform: Meta Ads (Facebook/Instagram)

Competitor Page URL: [Facebook page URL]
Main Products/Services: [E.g., CRM Software, Financial Consulting]

Oldest Active Ad (Library ID/URL): [Ad URL]
Run Time: 120 days
Creative Format: Video (18s) - Interface demo
Key Visual Elements: Software screens in use, growth charts, short testimonials.
Dominant Colors: Blue, gray, white (corporate palette)
Visual Hook (0-3s): Common problem ("Losing sales?") with a quick visual solution.

Headline: "Maximize your sales with our smart CRM!"
Primary Text: "Say goodbye to spreadsheets! Our CRM automates your sales funnel, manages customers, and gives you insights to grow. Try the difference. [link] #CRM #Sales #Automation"
Call-to-Action: "Try It Free"
Offer/Value Proposition: Free trial of CRM software focused on automation and sales growth.
Inferred Audience: Small and midsize business owners, sales managers, sales teams.

Notes: The competitor focuses on product demo videos, indicating an audience more familiar with the solution. The free trial CTA suggests a mid-funnel stage, aiming at direct user acquisition. The copy is concise and to the point, attacking the pain of manual inefficiency.
```

## Common Mistakes

1.  **Focusing only on direct, obvious competitors**: Ignoring indirect players, disruptive startups, or companies in adjacent niches can mean missing new tactics or innovative approaches. **How to avoid it**: Include in your monitoring list companies that solve the same problem in different ways or serve a similar audience with another product. Example: if you sell management software, don't just look at other software — also look at consultancies offering manual management or advanced spreadsheets that can act as substitutes.
