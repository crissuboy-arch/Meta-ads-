# Meta Ads Copywriting

Copy is never written from scratch — it's born from the real pains/objections collected in `research-[product].md`. If the research doesn't exist, don't write copy — ask to run `/research` first.

## Structure of each variation

- **Headline** (~40 characters max): direct benefit, or a question using the audience's real search terms.
- **Primary text** (80-125 characters before the "See more" cut, can continue after): opens with the hook (real pain or question), 1-2 solution sentences, social proof or a differentiator if available, clear CTA at the end.
- **Description** (link, optional): short reinforcement of the benefit or of real urgency (no manufactured urgency).
- **CTA (button)**: pick the native Meta button that best matches the objective (Shop Now, Learn More, Send Message, Sign Up) — never ask for an action the landing page doesn't deliver.

## The 3 angles per batch

Each `/creative` round generates 3 variations with different angles, all anchored to the SAME real offer (never invent an offer):

1. **Main pain**: opens with the most recurring objection/doubt from the research.
2. **Direct benefit**: opens with the result the product delivers, no beating around the bush.
3. **Social proof/authority**: opens with a review, customer count, credential, or real case (only if real data is available — never invent a testimonial or a number).

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **Nothing invented**: price, timeline, result, testimonial, or number that doesn't come from the config/research/user doesn't go in the copy.
2. **No empty clichés** ("quality and commitment," "the best on the market") without a fact to back them up.
3. **No individual-result promises** (health, money, aesthetics) — Meta rejects them and they're misleading. Swap "lose 22 lbs in 30 days" for "the method used by people who want results without the fluff," for example.
4. **1 CTA per variation**, always consistent with the campaign objective.
5. **The audience's language**, not the company's: use the exact terms that showed up in the research (that's how the audience already thinks about the problem).

## Regulated categories (extra care)

If the product is in **health, finance, employment, housing, or politics**, Meta has extra targeting and claim rules. In those cases: avoid any language that sounds like a diagnosis, a promise of financial return, or a guarantee of approval/result, and warn the user that the account may require additional verification.
